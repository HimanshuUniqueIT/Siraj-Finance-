Discussion

4.1  Introductions & group background

  • Siraj group: part of the Al Otaiba group of companies (a prominent Abu Dhabi family), with interests across banking, insurance, hospitality, real estate, IT and energy. Siraj Finance is a Central Bank–regulated entity.
  • Gibraltar Technologies (formerly HCL Infosystems) manages IT and infrastructure for the group across the Middle East (Abu Dhabi, Qatar, Bahrain, UAE) and India. Siraj Finance owns the functional / business requirements; GT owns technology and infrastructure.
  • Unique IT Solution: Ahmedabad-based; builds custom software and AI automation, mainly CRM/ERP for logistics, distribution, supply chain and manufacturing. Customers in India, plus Canada and the US. No existing UAE / Middle East clients; this is their first finance-sector CRM.

4.2  CRM functional walkthrough (against the BRD)

Unique IT Solution walked the ~15-step onboarding “sales journey” described in the BRD — the same path mapped screen-by-screen in the Customer Journey Presentation and the Customer Onboarding Flows:

  • Lead / inquiry capture from multiple sources → RM / sales-officer qualification → duplicate check → case-ID creation.
  • Product-specific document checklist → secure customer upload link (sent from the in-CRM communication hub) → customer uploads → RM tracks checklist completion.
  • AML / KYC screening → AI document review & summary (from documents + KYC findings).
  • Sales submission → credit / compliance review with risk rating; AI or KYC findings may be overridden with a mandatory reason and justification.
  • Credit approval → system drafts the Facility Offer Letter (FOL) from findings + product eligibility → FOL emailed to customer → customer accepts on the portal.
  • Issuance & disbursement of the loan / lending order → incentive update for RM / sales officer / supervisor.

Roles & hierarchy modelled: Head of Investment & Business, Head of Sales, Branch Supervisor, Senior RM, Sales Officer, Credit team, CAD, Trade Finance, Lending Operations, Internal Audit, System Administrator — each with its own dashboard and permissions, driven by role-based access control. Detailed per stakeholder in User Stories & Prerequisites.

Microsoft 365 Graph API: capture inbound and track outbound email; every RM–customer email tied to the customer timeline; RM email routing, attachments, access control, legal hold and auditing. Email / FOL / checklist templates (Sharia-compliant) to be provided by Siraj Finance.

Customer types: Individual and Corporate, with different document sets and KYC/AML programmes. Corporate onboarding checks guarantors, UBOs, directors and signatories. Trade-finance specifics (guarantee format, contract, PO) and LOS case generation are covered.

Also covered: centralised, searchable document repository (accepted letters, FOL snapshots & audit trails); English + Arabic multilingual readiness; incentive automation; role-based reporting & dashboards; immutability controls (a case cannot be edited after submission to credit; a locked final FOL cannot be edited after CAD lock); full audit trail on every stage transition; AECB credit-bureau integration via approved API.

4.3  AML / KYC & screening approach most-debated topic

Unique IT Solution’s stated position

The CRM will not perform screening. It integrates with Siraj Finance’s existing, compliant AML/CFT screening application via API and acts as a “postman”: it sends customer data (name, DOB, country code, address, etc.), receives real-time results (match / false-positive, with reasons), and displays and stores them in the CRM.

Claimed advantages: (1) no need to separately certify the CRM with the regulator / FIU / Sharia board, since it does not screen; (2) post-screening analytics, dashboards and case tracking live in the CRM while statutory reporting stays in the compliance system.

  • Screening scope named: sanctions, PEP, adverse media, UBO, geographic risk, source of funds / wealth, transaction monitoring — via approved third-party providers (LSEG / World-Check, Sumsub, LexisNexis; OFAC and other lists).
  • “AI limits”: AI findings must be reconciled against the screening system’s findings before a case proceeds.
  • GT’s clarification: is the CRM therefore only a capture-and-reporting layer, with all validation outside it? Unique IT: “partially right” — regulatory screening stays in the tried-and-tested system; the CRM adds AI and decision-making components on top. The policy engine (data localisation, etc.) remains with Siraj Finance’s software.
  • Deduplication is assumed to sit in the core-banking / AML system and be consumed via API — GT asked for this to be stated explicitly as an assumption.
  • Unique IT’s compliance specialist flagged ~49 open AML/KYC/checklist questions, parked for a session with Siraj Finance.

4.4  AI document intelligence & OCR

  • Extraction targets: bank statements (turnover, average balance, cheque returns), credit reports (score, liabilities), audited financials, corporate documents, guarantee / contract documents, customer applications; related parties; rules-based, producing an AI summary. Summary/report format to be defined by Siraj Finance in discovery.
  • OCR: PaddleOCR with their own models and logic — no external OCR API. Proven for English bank statements and credit reports (prior work extracting bank-statement data for a regulatory return).
  • Arabic is not yet tested. Unique IT’s view: largely a language-template / vocabulary matter; Arabic and mixed Arabic-English logic can be added.
  • GT pushback (Keval): not just a language pack — local dialects and case-by-case statement formats differ; this needs model training over time. Unique IT agreed to run experiments and establish an accuracy ratio.
  • GT (Keval): is the CRM compliant with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework? Unique IT: not currently; open to pursuing certification. Parked for later.

4.5  Credit workflow, CAD / FOL & Sharia compliance

  • Sales → credit handover; credit officer reviews the AI financial summary (average monthly income, net surplus, existing EMI, AI recommendation) plus credit reports; decision options: approve / approve-with-condition (pre-built, configurable conditions) / conditional decline.
  • Case routes to CAD (credit administration) for verification, then FOL generation. A Configuration Management module holds conditions, roles/permissions and per-product checklists.
  • FOL lifecycle: template library → draft → legal review against credit reports → issue to customer from the communication hub → acceptance → document perfection (CAD) → version control with a full audit trail of every edit. Sharia-compliant FOL template to be provided by Siraj Finance.
  • Sharia compliance — GT asked how it is ensured. Unique IT knows generic rules/benchmarks (e.g. EMI / loan amount / BG exception and customer identification printed on the FOL) — “half the glass” — and will rely on Siraj Finance to fill the rest; they will accommodate specifics.
  • Boundary of scope: Siraj Finance runs its own core banking / LOS for trade finance and lending. Unique IT’s scope ends at onboarding + CAD verification + offer acceptance; Siraj’s team then pushes the case into the LOS. The CRM retains data on disbursed amount vs conditional approval; disbursement itself happens in the LOS.

4.6  Scope, phasing & timeline

  • The original 10–12 month scope covers the full notification system (email + SMS + WhatsApp) and the enterprise feature set.
  • After the proposal was submitted, Siraj Finance asked (in a call with Unique IT) for the whole system in 6–7 months. Unique IT’s response: for that timeframe, deliver the email communication hub only (via M365 Graph API) and defer SMS & WhatsApp to Phase 2 (“enterprise version”).
  • GT reframed this as an MVP / phased approach and noted the 6–7 month plan is assumption-based, with assumptions still to be clarified.
  • No 6–7 month proposal has been submitted — this was the first meeting with any Siraj-side team since the proposal went in. Commercials on the table are for the full 10–12 month scope.

4.7  Navigational prototype

Unique IT demonstrated a clickable navigational prototype built on their reading of the BRD, tracing the full journey: inquiry capture → lead stages (new / called / connected / qualified) → case ID → product-based secure upload link → checklist tracking → AI document review → documents fetched via government APIs where available (Emirates ID, credit report) or uploaded manually (e.g. bank statements) → AI summary → compliance / AML officer → risk-rating computation (parameters to be supplied by Siraj Finance) → consolidated report with override → credit officer decision → CAD verification → FOL → customer acceptance on portal → data handoff to LOS. The customer portal shows the full timeline from day one. It follows the same path as the Customer Journey Presentation.

GT questions raised: which deduplication algorithm (proprietary vs core-banking); and how the far side of each government API is authenticated / secured. Unique IT: government authorities provide subscription-based API access that banks already hold, and they build to each authority’s API spec and certification (e.g. AECB / Experian / CIBIL). GT: still an open question — to be resolved in the discovery phase.

4.9  Cloud choice — AWS vs Azure vs on-prem

  • GT (Satish): with a Microsoft .NET stack and M365 / Exchange for mail, why AWS and not Azure? Concerns: cost, single-OEM support, avoiding a single point of failure and a support model that needs both AWS and Microsoft specialists.
  • Unique IT: Azure is equally viable — the design is portable and every AWS service used has an Azure equivalent. AWS was the default from their own experience; the customer’s choice; they will evaluate and assist.
  • Agreed: Unique IT to prepare Azure and on-prem options with architecture and cost, plus an indicative recurring cloud-subscription comparison (AWS vs Azure/Microsoft).
  • On-prem is offered; it needs Siraj Finance load / footfall data (last 5 years + next 5 years) for capacity planning.
  • Timeline impact of a stack change: Unique IT expects the backend to be largely unaffected, with a variation window of up to ~1–1.5 months (they asked GT to cap it near 1 month). GT wants this captured as a risk & mitigation item with an impact assessment. GT’s stated intent is to reduce cost for Siraj Finance.

4.10  Non-functional requirements

  • Security: 2FA, SSO readiness, password policy, session timeout, device / IP monitoring.
  • Access control: role-based per department; case-state-based permissions.
  • Audit trail: per-stage customer timeline; change tracking on rules and templates.
  • Data protection: masking of PII / sensitive fields (not visible from the back end or to unprivileged roles), per regulator. GT asked Unique IT to check UAE data-protection norms and state compliance (yes/no) as an evaluation checklist item, and to review the GT/Siraj compliance document and gap-fill.
  • Availability: production system plus backups and disaster recovery.
  • Integration: API-ready architecture for M365, SMS/WhatsApp, credit bureau and AML/sanctions vendors; configurable multi-provider (add URL / port / credentials → system fetches findings); integration-ready for LSEG, Sumsub, LexisNexis, OFAC / World-Check lists.
  • Hosting: enterprise-grade cloud or on-prem (both options), subject to approval by Siraj Finance’s security team.
  • Change control: template, workflow and permission changes require formal approval and version control.

4.11  Support model, SLA, warranty & commercials

  • SLA: not yet defined. Unique IT wants to align to Siraj Finance’s expectations first (L1/L2/L3 and P1/P2/P3 classification is usually the customer’s ITSM call). GT noted the customer may need educating on tier definitions, but the vendor cannot contradict the customer’s severity classification. Logged as an open discussion item; Unique IT is flexible.
  • Warranty: 90 days post go-live — bug fixes, performance monitoring, minor functional corrections, production-issue resolution. Then sign-off, and AMC begins (T&M / resource-based / bug-based options).
  • Change requests / enhancements: BRD → proposal / PO → sign-off → new version under version control.
  • UAT: scope document includes a UAT period and UAT sign-off support.
  • RACI matrix: not yet prepared — needs sessions with GT / actual users to map module ownership. Open item for the final proposal.
  • Milestones: each phase is a milestone. Phase 1 = core CRM modules (customer management, onboarding, branch & sales hierarchy, reports & dashboards, roles & permissions), delivered to a SIT environment. GT wants far more detail per milestone: deliverables, acceptance criteria, turnaround / waiting time, a discovery / requirement-specification document with sign-off, onsite headcount, prerequisites, environment readiness, and a Gantt / concise phase summary.
  • Commercials: average rate ~USD 11 / hour. GT could not fully reconcile the totals against a 7-month timeline (~10 months at 5 resources depending on staffing). Unique IT: PM and Business Analyst are not charged; billing is for UI/UX, senior .NET backend, frontend and AI developers, on a critical-path basis, not all full-time; hours billed against work. GT was comfortable with this.
  • Blended rate for future change requests is not in the proposal. Unique IT to discuss internally and provide if possible.
